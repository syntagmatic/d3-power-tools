Warming World

140 years of global temperature anomalies, told in five chapters. Scroll to explore.

The Baseline

Temperature anomalies measure deviation from a reference period — the 1901–2000 average. Zero doesn't mean cold; it means normal for the 20th century.

A Stable Century

From 1884 to ~1940, anomalies hovered between −0.4°C and −0.1°C. Fluctuations existed, but no clear trend emerged. The climate system appeared in equilibrium.

The Acceleration

Starting in the 1970s, something changed. Anomalies crossed zero and kept climbing. Blue years gave way to red. By 2000, the positive trend was unmistakable — each decade warmer than the last.

Record-Breaking Years

The hottest years cluster in the last two decades. 2016 and 2023 shattered records at over +1.0°C. What was once extraordinary became the new normal.

The Trend Accelerates

Decade-by-decade, the warming rate itself is increasing. The slope from 1970–2000 was +0.18°C/decade. Since 2000, it has risen to +0.24°C/decade. The curve bends upward.

Data are synthetic but modeled on NASA GISS global temperature anomaly records. Baseline: 1901–2000 average.
