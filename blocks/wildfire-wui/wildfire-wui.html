Where wildfire meets suburbia

The wildland-urban interface—the WUI—is where human development presses against undeveloped wildland. It’s the fastest-growing land use type in America.

The interface

The WUI isn’t a thin line—it’s 432,000 square miles of land where homes and wildland intermingle. That’s larger than Texas and California combined.

It’s not where you think

WUI isn’t just mountain cabins. Suburban sprawl has pushed the boundary into places most people don’t think of as fire country: the Front Range, the Florida panhandle, the Ozarks.

Colorado’s Front Range

Boulder County sits at the junction of the Great Plains and the Rocky Mountain foothills. Subdivisions climb into canyons thick with drought-stressed grass. On December 30, 2021, it became ground zero.

Building faster, building closer

Since 1990, WUI housing has nearly doubled while the rest of the country grew by just 22%. Forty-three percent of all new homes are now built in the interface.

A diverging story

WUI housing grew 96% from 1990 to 2020. Non-WUI housing grew just 22%. The gap keeps widening.

The Western surge

Nevada (+112%), Utah (+98%), Arizona (+91%), and Colorado (+84%) lead the nation in WUI housing growth. These are also the states seeing the worst fire seasons.

Accelerating

The WUI’s share of new housing starts has climbed from 28% in 1990 to 43% today. We are not just building in the fire zone—we are building there faster.

The asymmetry of defense

When fire threatens structures, everything changes. Crews shift from managing fire to protecting homes. The cost per acre jumps elevenfold.

December 30, 2021

A grass fire near Marshall Road. Winds gusting to 105 mph. Temperature 54°F—in winter. The Marshall Fire would become the most destructive in Colorado history, not by acres but by structures: 1,084 homes destroyed in a matter of hours.

The market sees what policy doesn’t

Insurers are fleeing WUI counties. In 2023, 2.3 million policies were not renewed nationwide. FAIR Plan enrollments—the insurer of last resort—have nearly tripled since 2018. And yet building permits keep coming.

The exodus

Policy non-renewals have grown 7x since 2018. Florida and California lead, but the trend is spreading to Colorado, Oregon, Montana, and Louisiana.

Last resort, first choice

When private insurers leave, homeowners turn to state FAIR Plans. These plans were designed as temporary backstops, not primary coverage. California’s FAIR Plan now covers 380,000 policies—and growing.

Building despite the signal

Even in states with the highest non-renewal rates, WUI building permits haven’t slowed. The insurance market is pricing risk that zoning boards aren’t.
