The Baseline

Global temperature anomalies measure how much warmer or cooler each year is compared to the 1951–1980 average. A value of +0.5°C means that year was half a degree warmer than the baseline period.

The blue band marks the baseline era used as the reference point.

A Stable Century

For nearly a century, temperatures fluctuated within a narrow band. Major volcanic eruptions—like Santa María (1902) and Agung (1963)—injected aerosols that briefly cooled the planet.

Year-to-year variation was real, but the long-term trend was essentially flat.

The Acceleration

Starting around 1970, the line shifts from blue (below baseline) to red (above baseline). This transition from cool to warm anomalies is the fingerprint of accelerating greenhouse gas emissions.

Record Breaking

The last decade contains the hottest years ever recorded. The bold line shows the 10-year moving average, smoothing out year-to-year noise to reveal the relentless upward march.

The Trend

A linear regression from 1970 to 2023 quantifies the acceleration. The slope tells us exactly how fast we are warming—and the most recent data point shows where we stand today.
