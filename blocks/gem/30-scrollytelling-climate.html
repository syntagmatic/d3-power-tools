The Baseline

Global temperature anomaly measures how much warmer or colder it is compared to a long-term average. Here, we use the 1951-1980 baseline (0°C).

From 1884 to the mid-20th century, temperatures fluctuated slightly below this baseline, representing a relatively stable period in Earth's recent climate history.

A Stable Century

Zooming into 1884-1970, we see a world before rapid acceleration. While the climate was naturally variable, major events left their mark.

Notice the dips around 1902 and 1963? These correspond to massive volcanic eruptions (Santa Maria and Agung), which pumped ash into the stratosphere, temporarily cooling the planet.

The Acceleration

Starting around 1970, the pattern changes dramatically. The line crosses the baseline and never looks back.

What was once a mix of cooler (blue) and warmer (red) years becomes a relentless climb. This shift marks the clear signal of human-induced global warming.

Record Breaking

The last 10 years have been the warmest on record. The top 5 years are all very recent.

The bold line shows the 10-year moving average, smoothing out the year-to-year noise to reveal the underlying momentum of our warming world.

The Trend

Since 1970, the warming trend has been remarkably linear and steep. The slope represents a warming of approximately +0.20°C per decade.

Without significant intervention, this trend is set to continue, pushing us into uncharted territory.
