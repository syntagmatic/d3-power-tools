The Baseline

Global temperature anomalies measure deviation from the 1951–1980 average. The blue band marks this reference period.

A value of +0.5 °C means that year was half a degree warmer than the three-decade average.

A Stable Century

For nearly a hundred years, temperatures fluctuated in a narrow band. Volcanic eruptions like Santa María (1902) and Mt. Agung (1963) injected aerosols that briefly cooled the planet.

Year-to-year variation was real, but the long-term trend was essentially flat.

The Acceleration

Starting around 1970, the color shifts from blue to red. Cool anomalies give way to warm ones—the fingerprint of accelerating greenhouse gas emissions.

Each bar encodes the anomaly: blue below baseline, red above.

Record-Breaking Years

The five hottest years on record cluster in the last decade. A 10-year moving average (bold line) smooths annual noise and reveals the relentless upward march.

The Trend by Decade

A regression line from 1970 onward quantifies the warming rate. The slope steepens with each passing decade.

We are not just warming—we are warming faster.
