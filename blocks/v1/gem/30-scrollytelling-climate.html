1. The Baseline

Climate scientists use a "baseline" to measure change. The period from 1951 to 1980 (blue band) represents a global average temperature that was stable enough to serve as our zero-point reference.

Every data point shows the "anomaly"—how much warmer or cooler that year was compared to this stable era.

2. A Stable Century

For most of the late 19th and early 20th centuries, temperatures fluctuated but showed no long-term warming trend. In fact, major volcanic eruptions often led to multi-year cooling periods.

Events like Agung (1963) injected aerosols into the stratosphere, briefly shielding the planet from solar radiation.

3. The Acceleration

Around 1970, the pattern changed. As greenhouse gas emissions surged, global temperatures broke away from the historic range. The line shifts from cool blues to warming reds.

The bold line shows the 10-year moving average, smoothing out year-to-year noise to reveal the underlying momentum.

4. Record Breaking

The acceleration has reached unprecedented levels. The last ten years have been the warmest since records began in 1880.

Every single one of the top 5 warmest years has occurred in the last decade, with 2023 shattering previous records.

5. The Trend Line

If we look at the trend since 1970, the slope is unmistakable. We are currently warming at a rate of approximately 0.20°C per decade.

This steady upward march defines the challenge of the 21st century as we move further and further from the 20th-century baseline.
